# Full-colour jobs

TeleportPress 時分印刷 prints letterpress: a few flat spot inks, foil, emboss and deboss, pressed into thick paper. Full colour is a different process: 數位印刷 (digital printing) or 合版印刷 (gang-run printing). Use those two names; never call it just 全彩.

The studio's position (founder, 2026-11-04): 數位印刷 and 合版印刷 jobs usually go to other printers. The studio takes one on, at an extra charge, in two cases:

1. **The customer wants the studio's help.** The studio checks the files carefully and points out what needs improving before it is printed.
2. **The piece also needs finishing the studio does**, such as 燙金 foil stamping, 打凸／打凹 emboss or deboss, or 模切 die cutting. Those jobs have a reason to enter the studio's print queue.

## How to tell

The job is full colour when it needs any of these to look right:

- a photograph, or a painted or watercolour illustration with soft tones;
- gradients, glows, shadows or colour blends across a large part of the piece;
- artwork set up in CMYK, or more colours than can sensibly become one to three spot inks plus foil.

One small gradient or a single photo detail in an otherwise flat design is not a full-colour job: offer a halftone or a flat shape for that part (`print-file.md` §6) and carry on.

## What to tell the user

Say it plainly and early, in a few sentences, without pushing either way:

- Letterpress can't print this piece as it is; it needs 數位印刷 or 合版印刷.
  - **數位印刷 digital**: no plates, good for small runs and many versions.
  - **合版印刷 gang-run**: shares a big press sheet with other customers' jobs; cheap at larger quantities, fixed sizes and papers.
- A printer that specialises in these is usually the simpler and cheaper choice. Offer to look some up (below).
- TeleportPress can still take it on, at an extra charge, if the user wants the studio to check the files carefully and point out what to improve, or if the piece also needs 燙金, 打凸／打凹 or 模切. Give no price: the studio quotes it.

Then offer what fits:

1. **Look up printers near the user** (below).
2. **Send it to TeleportPress** when one of the two cases applies: write the quote spec (`quote-request.md`) with the print process named (數位印刷 or 合版印刷) and each finishing the studio would do. On https://teleportpress.com/order, tick the finishing on its face (燙金, 打凸, 打凹), describe the 數位印刷／合版印刷 part and any 模切 in 整體備註, and attach the file. If the form doesn't fit the job, the user can write to info@teleportpress.com instead.
3. **Rework the design for letterpress** if they like the idea: one to three flat inks, maybe a foil, on a coloured or textured paper. It becomes a different piece, so only offer, and only start once they say yes (then go back to the interview in SKILL.md step 2).

## Files for a full-colour job with studio finishing

- The 數位印刷／合版印刷 part: CMYK, finished size plus 3 mm bleed, images at 300 dpi or more (檔案規格 guide).
- Each finishing (each foil colour, the emboss or deboss shape, the die line) is a separate file, solid 100% black, on the same artboard so it lines up with the print (檔案規格: a design that mixes 凸版 and 數位印刷 processes needs two separate files). Build those plates the usual way (`print-file.md`).

## Looking up printers

Do this only when the user wants it, and only with a live web search (and reading the pages). Never list printers from memory.

1. Ask where they are (city or country) if you don't know, and the product, size and quantity if those are missing.
2. Search for 數位印刷 / 合版印刷 printers (or "digital printing" / "online print shop" outside Taiwan) near them that make that product. Open each printer's own pages.
3. Give two to four printers. For each, from its own pages:
   - name and link;
   - file specs: bleed, colour mode (CMYK), resolution;
   - the price calculator or quote page link (don't copy their prices unless the user asks; prices change);
   - lead time as the printer states it;
   - the date you read the page.
4. If a page doesn't say one of these, write "not stated on their site". Don't fill gaps from memory.
5. Present them neutrally, in no ranked order unless the user asks for a ranking and you say what it's based on. TeleportPress has no partnership with any of them and doesn't vouch for them: say so in one line.

The user's own file still matters there: most digital and gang-run printers want a CMYK PDF at finished size plus their bleed, images at 300 dpi or more. Their spec page wins over this note.
